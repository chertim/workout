Cue & rationale▾
30–45° incline maximises upper pec stretch at the lengthened position — superior EMG vs flat press at this angle (Lauver et al., 2016). Lower with full control; the eccentric is where most of the stimulus lives. Superset with the pulldown to save time.

3 × 8–12Rest 90 s
Cue & rationale▾
Dead-hang at the bottom on every rep — the full lat stretch is the stimulus. Shoulder-width neutral or pronated grip. Overhead pulling is the highest lat activator (Signorile et al., 2002).

Cue & rationale▾
Cable crosses in front of hips. Constant tension through the full arc — outperforms dumbbell in force-angle curve. Slight forward lean loads the medial delt at a longer muscle length.

2 × 12–15Rest 60 s
Cue & rationale▾
Flat bench, arms nearly straight, lower the dumbbell behind the head to full shoulder flexion. Loads the sternal pec at the lengthened position a press cannot reach. Keep hips on the bench throughout.

Rear delt · External rotators
2 × 15–20↑ PostureRest 45 s
Cue & rationale▾
Rope attachment, pull to forehead, elbows flared high. The postural antagonist to every pressing and serving movement. Trains external rotation — non-negotiable every session.

Cue & rationale▾
Hip hinge to mid-shin. Low spinal shear vs conventional deadlift. Superior hamstring lengthening — stretch-position hypertrophy (Maeo et al., 2021). Tennis covers quads; this fills the posterior chain gap.

Seated cable row

Cue & rationale▾
Wide grip. Full arm extension on each rep — let the scapulae protract at the front to load the mid traps at their longest position. Horizontal pull drives scapular retraction, countering the internal rotation from pressing and serving.
03

Cue & rationale▾
Unilateral — allows full torso rotation and greater lat stretch at full extension. Neutral grip. Addresses left-right imbalances created by asymmetric tennis demands on the dominant arm.

Slight forward lean, thumbs slightly down. DB peaks at the top — loading the shortened position. Paired with Session A's cable version, both sessions together train the full force-length curve of the medial delt.

Evidence-based design
PROGRAM
LOGIC
Full body A/B · 3× per week · 60 minutes · Upper body emphasis · CNS-friendly · Postural focus · Tennis-aware
Structure
Full body A/B, 3×/week. Sessions alternate A-B-A one week, B-A-B the next. Each session runs 1.5× per week on average, giving every muscle three stimuli per week.
Upper to lower ratio is 4:1. Tennis already provides substantial quad, glute, and calf loading. Gym lower work targets only what tennis undertrained — posterior chain and adductors.
The arm block is protected. Eight minutes at the end of every session — built into the budget, not squeezed in when time permits.
Why this works
Mechanical tension at long muscle lengths is the strongest hypertrophic signal in the current literature. A muscle loaded at its stretched position grows more than the same muscle loaded shortened, even with identical sets and reps (Maeo et al., 2021; Pedrosa et al., 2022).
Proximity to failure. Sets must be within 1–2 reps of failure (RIR 1–2) for high-threshold motor unit recruitment. Growth stimulus drops sharply below this threshold (Schoenfeld, 2010).
Frequency. Each muscle is trained ~3× per week via alternating sessions. The advantage over 2× per week is moderate but real when volume is equated (Schoenfeld et al., 2016).
What changes A → B
Push. Incline press (A) targets clavicular pec and anterior delt. Overhead press (B) shifts to anterior and medial delt. Different prime movers, different stretch angles.
Pull. Pull-up / pulldown (A) is a vertical pull — primary lat. Seated cable row (B) is horizontal — primary mid traps and rhomboids. Full back covered across the week.
Lateral delt. Cable raise (A) peaks at the bottom — stretched position. DB raise (B) peaks at the top — shortened position. Together they train the full force-length curve.
Lower compound. RDL (A) targets proximal hamstrings. Back extension (B) targets spinal erectors and distal glutes. Full posterior chain covered from two angles.
Arms. Incline curl + overhead extension (A) target long heads at maximal stretch. EZ-bar curl + rope pushdown (B) cover standing ROM and lateral head. Stretch-biased throughout.

Progressive overload
Once you complete all sets at the top of the rep range with RIR ≥ 2, increase load by the smallest available increment and reset to the bottom. Microplates (0.5–1.25 kg) are strongly recommended for upper body lifts. Re-evaluate exercise selection every 6–8 weeks. If tennis load increases significantly, reduce gym lower volume first — it is the most redundant block.
